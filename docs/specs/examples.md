# Example

Here we examine the principles of bookkeeping from three perspectives:

- Tracing transaction flows between accounts.
- Record transactions as journal entries
- Financial statements

To illustrate the flow, here is the scenario involving a hypothetical UK private limited company, **ACME Private Limited**—a **small manufacturing** business that produces bespoke polymer parts on **FDM 3D printers**. Alice and Bob are founding shareholders. ACME’s principal **property, plant and equipment** is **3D printers**, **factory fit-out** (leasehold improvements to its manufacturing unit), and **office equipment** (administration). It employs **two production operators** on payroll.

## Post to Ledger

The ledger accounts summarise the activity to find the final balances as of 30 June 202X:

- **Bank current account (EFG) - 1010:** £1,000 + £10,000 + £40,000 + £4,000 + £2,500 − £200 − £20,950 − £100 − £900 − £855 − £5 = **£34,490 (Debit)**
- **Petty Cash - 1020:** £200 − £50 = **£150 (Debit)**
- **Trade debtors - 1030:** £2,500 − £2,500 = **£0 (Debit)**
- **Office equipment - 1500:** £50 + £1,450 = **£1,500 (Debit)**
- **Plant & machinery – 3D printers - 1510:** **£12,000 (Debit)**
- **Leasehold improvements – factory - 1520:** **£7,500 (Debit)**
- **Accum. depreciation - 1501:** **£250 (Credit)**
- **PAYE and NICs payable - 2010:** £100 + £95 = **£195 (Credit)**
- **Trade creditors - 2020:** £100 − £100 = **£0 (Credit)**
- **Bank borrowings (EFG) - 2100:** **£40,000 (Credit)**
- **Share capital - 3010 & 3020:** **£11,000 (Credit)**
- **Turnover - 4010:** £4,000 + £2,500 = **£6,500 (Credit)**
- **Staff costs - 5010:** £1,000 + £950 = **£1,950 (Debit)**
- **Stationery and office costs - 5040:** **£100 (Debit)**
- **Depreciation - 5020:** **£250 (Debit)**
- **Interest on borrowings - 5030:** **£5 (Debit)**

**Check balance:**
Assets (£34,490 + £150 + £0 + £1,500 + £12,000 + £7,500 − £250) = **£55,390**
Liabilities (£195 + £0 + £40,000) + Equity (£11,000 + £6,500 − £1,950 − £100 − £250 − £5) = **£55,390**
